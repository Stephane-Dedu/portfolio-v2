## After AWS and Terraform, Kubernetes

With the SAA and the Terraform Associate, I knew how to design an AWS infrastructure and describe it as code. But during my apprenticeship at Swapn, our microservices ran on Kubernetes, deployed with Helm, and it was clear that a big part of production happened there.

So I went for the **CKA (Certified Kubernetes Administrator)**. After the Terraform Associate lesson, I wanted a certification that wouldn't leave me staring at a blank page.

## No multiple choice, just a terminal

90% of IT certifications don't prove much in the field. The CKA is one of the rare exceptions: it's 100% hands-on. You're dropped into a terminal with broken clusters and a countdown, and the goal is to debug and fix everything.

It's stressful, but honestly it was the most fun and formative exam I've taken. Memorizing won't save you: either you can read a `kubectl describe` and trace back to the cause, or the clock runs out. It builds real reflexes.

## How I prepared

Two resources were enough:

- **KodeKloud** for the fundamentals and guided labs
- **Killer.sh** for exam simulations, harder than the real exam, which is exactly what you want

Unlike the Terraform Associate, I spent most of my time in the terminal rather than in the documentation. That was the right lesson to apply.

## The result

Certified in March 2026. Beyond the badge, I read a cluster very differently now: the control plane, the kubelet, pod networking and storage are no longer black boxes. It's also what made me want to explore the security side with the CKS.
